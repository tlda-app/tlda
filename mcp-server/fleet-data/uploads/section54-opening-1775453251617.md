# Section 5.4 opening — draft

## What I think the opening needs to do

The section title already says "inner product perspective." The reader coming from 5.3 doesn't need to be told "we were doing X, now we're doing Y." They need the decomposition and what it buys them.

The existing post-equation text (lines 1392–1400) is good — it explains the negligible second term, the Cauchy-Schwarz path, the balancing path, the orthogonality advantage. The opening just needs to get us to the equation.

## Draft

> The AIPW bias decomposes into an inner product of the weight error $\delta\gamma = \hat\gamma - \gamma^{ipw}$ and the outcome error $\delta m$, plus the imbalance in $\delta m$ at the true weights:

Then the equation. Then the existing text starting with "The second term is the imbalance in $\delta m$..."

## Why this works

- No meta-commentary about "preceding subsections" or "complementary perspectives"
- Flows directly into the equation with a verb ("decomposes")
- Names the two errors that will drive the rest of the section
- Lets the post-equation text do the interpretive work (which it already does well)
- The connection to maximal imbalance comes naturally in paragraph 3 (line 1398): "The balancing arguments in Sections 5.1 and 5.2 take a different path..."

## What it doesn't do

- It doesn't explain why the inner product perspective matters before showing it — but the section is short and the "why" emerges from the Cauchy-Schwarz/orthogonality discussion that follows immediately. Front-loading the motivation was what made every previous attempt sound like a topic shift.
