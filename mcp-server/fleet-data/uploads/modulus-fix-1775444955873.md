# Modulus definition fix — two options

## Current (wrong)

$$\omega(\delta) = \sup\left\{|\mathbb{E}(m_1(X)) - \mathbb{E}(m_2(X))| : m_1, m_2 \in \mathcal{M},\; \|m_1 - m_2\|_{L_2(P)} \le \delta\right\}$$

Then: "At the optimal bias–variance tradeoff parameterized by $s > 0$, $\text{imbalance}(\hat\gamma) = \omega(s) - \omega'(s)\,s$."

Wrong because: $P$ should be $P_n$, and the bias formula $\omega(s)-\omega'(s)s$ belongs to the penalized form, not the constrained form.

---

## Option A: Constrained form (Donoho/Armstrong style)

**Definition:**
$$\omega(\delta) = \sup\left\{\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_i m_1(X_i) - \frac{1}{n}\sum_i m_2(X_i)\right| : m_1, m_2 \in \mathcal{M},\; \left\|\,m_1 - m_2\,\right\|_{L_2(P_n)} \le \delta\right\}$$

which measures how much the empirical mean can change when the underlying function changes by $\delta$ in empirical $L_2$ norm.

**Bias formula:** The bias of the minimax linear estimator is
$$\text{imbalance}_{\mathcal{M}}(\hat\gamma) \le \frac{\omega(\bar\delta)}{2}$$
where $\bar\delta$ is determined by the bias–variance tradeoff.

**Approximate linearity:** When $\omega(\delta) \approx c\,\delta$, the half-modulus $\omega(\bar\delta)/2$ at the optimal $\bar\delta$ is small relative to $n^{-1/2}$. (Less clean — the "approximately zero" punchline requires more explanation because $\omega(\bar\delta)/2$ is not *zero* when $\omega$ is linear; it's that the optimal tradeoff makes $\bar\delta$ small.)

**Pro:** Familiar from Donoho (1994), Armstrong & Kolesár (2018). Readers who know this literature recognize it instantly.

**Con:** The bias formula $\omega(\bar\delta)/2$ is less elegant than $\omega(s) - \omega'(s)s$, and the "linearity implies zero bias" intuition doesn't land as cleanly. You'd need to explain: when $\omega$ is linear, the half-modulus at *any* $\bar\delta$ is $c\bar\delta/2$, and the optimal $\bar\delta$ is driven to zero by the variance term — so the bias vanishes. That's an extra step.

---

## Option B: Penalized form (Bregman paper style)

**Definition:**
$$\omega(s) = \sup_{m \in \mathcal{M}} \left\{ \frac{1}{n}\sum_i m(X_i) - \frac{s}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_{W_i=1} m(X_i)^2 \right\}$$

which measures the worst-case empirical mean over the model, penalized by the empirical $L_2$ norm on the treated sample. The parameter $s$ controls the bias–variance tradeoff.

**Bias formula:**
$$\text{imbalance}_{\mathcal{M}}(\hat\gamma) = \omega(s) - \omega'(s)\,s$$

at the optimal tradeoff $s > 0$.

**Approximate linearity:** When $\omega(s) \approx c\,s$ for some constant $c$, the bias $\omega(s) - \omega'(s)\,s \approx cs - cs = 0$. Clean punchline — linearity of $\omega$ means zero bias, full stop.

**Pro:** The bias formula $\omega(s) - \omega'(s)s$ works directly with the definition. The "linearity $\Rightarrow$ zero bias" punchline is immediate. Connects directly to the Bregman paper.

**Con:** Less familiar. Readers who know Donoho/Armstrong may not immediately recognize this as the modulus. The penalized form also looks less like a "modulus of continuity" — the name suggests a constrained form.

---

## My read

Option B is more internally consistent — the definition, bias formula, and linearity intuition all fit together without extra steps. But Option A is what the literature uses. A possible hybrid: define it in the constrained form (Option A) for familiarity, but then *immediately* note that by Lagrangian duality, the bias at the optimal tradeoff is $\omega(s) - \omega'(s)s$ for the corresponding penalized parameter $s$. That gives the clean punchline without abandoning the standard definition.

Either way, the fix is $P \to P_n$.
