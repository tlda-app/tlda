# Modulus math — formulas, bounds, approximations {#latest}

## Setup

Constrained modulus:

$$\omega(\delta) = \sup\left\{\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_i m_1(X_i) - \frac{1}{n}\sum_i m_2(X_i)\right| : m_1, m_2 \in \mathcal{M},\; \|m_1-m_2\|_{L_2(\hat P^{W=1})} \le \sigma\delta\right\}$$

Bias at operating point $\delta$:

$$b(\delta) = \tfrac{1}{2}\{\omega(\delta) - \delta\,\omega'(\delta)\}$$

Variance:

$$v(\delta)^2 = \sigma^2[\omega'(\delta)/2]^2$$

## 1. What characterizes $\delta_\sigma$

FOC for MSE-optimal $\delta$ at noise level $\sigma$:

$$\delta\,[\omega(\delta) - \delta\,\omega'(\delta)] = \sigma^2\,\omega'(\delta)$$

Equivalently: $\delta \cdot 2b(\delta) = \sigma^2 \omega'(\delta)$, so

$$b(\delta_\sigma) = \frac{\sigma^2\,\omega'(\delta_\sigma)}{2\,\delta_\sigma}$$

The bias at the operating point equals $\sigma^2$ times the slope-to-distance ratio.

## 2. Decomposition

Write $\omega(\delta) = c\,\delta + \xi(\delta)$ where $c = \omega'(0^+)$ and $\xi$ is the offset complexity (deviation from linearity). Then $\omega'(\delta) = c + \xi'(\delta)$ and

$$b(\delta) = \tfrac{1}{2}[\xi(\delta) - \delta\,\xi'(\delta)]$$

The linear part $c\delta$ cancels. Bias depends only on $\xi$.

Properties of $\xi$: concave, $\xi(0) = 0$, $\xi \ge 0$. So $\xi(\delta)/\delta$ is decreasing and $\xi(\delta) - \delta\xi'(\delta) \ge 0$.

## 3. FOC in terms of offset complexity

Substituting into the FOC:

$$\delta[\xi(\delta) - \delta\xi'(\delta)] = \sigma^2[c + \xi'(\delta)]$$

In the linear region ($\xi \approx 0$, $\xi' \approx 0$): LHS $\approx 0$, RHS $= \sigma^2 c > 0$. **No solution.** So $\delta_\sigma$ is where $\xi$ starts to curve.

## 4. Bound on offset complexity (Bregman paper)

The offset complexity is bounded by the localized Gaussian width:

$$\xi(\delta) \le C\,r^2 \quad \text{for } \delta \text{ in the range } [0, \delta_{\max}]$$

where $r$ is the fixed-point radius satisfying

$$\frac{r^2}{2} \ge \sup_{\substack{h \in \mathcal{M},\; \|h\|_{L_2} \le r}} (\hat P - P)\{h(X)(1 - W\gamma^{\text{ipw}}(X)\,h(X))\}$$

(the empirical process localization, centered at $\gamma^{\text{ipw}}$).

The approximation condition enters here: the centering at $\gamma^{\text{ipw}}$ requires $\gamma^{\text{ipw}} \approx g \in \mathcal{M}$ so that $h \cdot \gamma^{\text{ipw}} \approx h \cdot g$ stays in the model's complexity class. Specifically:

$$\|g\|_\mathcal{M} \le C\,r^2\,n/\sigma^2, \qquad \|g - \gamma^{\text{ipw}}\|_{L_2} \le C\,r^2\,\sqrt{n}/\sigma$$

## 5. Bias bound

Since $\xi$ is concave and bounded by $Cr^2$, the tangent intercept satisfies

$$b(\delta_\sigma) = \tfrac{1}{2}[\xi(\delta_\sigma) - \delta_\sigma\xi'(\delta_\sigma)] \le C\,r^2$$

(using: for concave $\xi$ with $0 \le \xi \le \nu$, the tangent intercept $\xi - \delta\xi' \le 2\nu$.)

## 6. For Sobolev/RKHS models

Fixed-point radius: $r \asymp n^{-s/(2s+d)}$ where $s$ = smoothness, $d$ = dimension.

$$b(\delta_\sigma) \lesssim r^2 \asymp n^{-2s/(2s+d)} = o(n^{-1/2}) \text{ when } s > d/2$$

The approximation condition: for nonparametric models with constant $\sigma$, the norm budget $Cr^2 n/\sigma^2 \asymp n^{d/(2s+d)} \to \infty$. Since the model is nonparametric, $\gamma^{\text{ipw}}$ can be approximated at gauge $\le B$ with $L_2$ error $\to 0$ as $B \to \infty$. The shrinking error budget $Cr^2\sqrt{n}/\sigma \asymp n^{(d-2s)/(2(2s+d))} \to 0$ for $s > d/2$ is beaten by the improving approximation.

## 7. What we can say without knowing $\gamma^{\text{ipw}}$

We can compute $\omega(\delta)$ from the model and data. We know $\sigma$ (we chose it). The FOC $\delta[\omega(\delta)-\delta\omega'(\delta)] = \sigma^2\omega'(\delta)$ gives us $\delta_\sigma$.

But: $\omega$ depends on the sample, and the bias bound $Cr^2$ depends on $r$ which comes from localization. Both are computable from the model.

What's NOT computable: the exact bias $b(\delta_\sigma)$, because it depends on $\gamma^{\text{ipw}}$ through the offset complexity. But the BOUND $b \le Cr^2$ is computable (from the model and $n$), and it tells us $\delta_\sigma$ is in the region where the shaded gap in the figure is at most $Cr^2$.
